How Fast May a Risk Model React? Margin Procyclicality as a Feedback Loop

SSRN Working Paper | Co-Authors: Achyut Kumar, Ishanth Goli, Abhinav Duddala, Aarnav Bujamella (Sept 2026)
Read on SSRN
  • Closed-Form Speed Limit & Bifurcation Theory: Models central clearing margin requirements and constrained trader order flow as a closed-loop dynamical system with loop gain g = λQ/m and realized volatility amplified by 1/(1 − g). Derives the exact stability threshold θγg* < 1 − g* and proves that loss of stability occurs via a Neimark–Sacker bifurcation producing a quasi-periodic margin cycle of period 3 to 4 updating intervals.
  • Estimator Design & Mark-to-Market Capital Channel: Demonstrates that risk-model estimator shape matters more than lookback length—an equal-weight window (used in SPAN-style systems) tolerates only one-quarter of the loop gain of an exponential filter with the same mean lag—and that marking trader capital to market tightens the stability limit by an additional factor of 1 − g*.
  • Quiet-Period Risk, Margin Floors & Structural Estimation: Identifies that in high-gain markets, calm stretches lower perceived variance, raise leverage, and push the gain toward the no-clearing boundary (g ≥ 1), proving that a margin floor at √(λW) eliminates this failure mode exactly. Develops a maximum-likelihood estimator to recover loop gain directly from return series and published margin rules.
Abstract Summary — SSRN Publication (Abstract ID: 7501698)
“A clearing house sets margin from a risk model that reads prices, and the traders who post that margin move the prices the model reads. We close the loop and derive the speed at which a risk model may react before it destabilizes the market it measures. The loop gain is g = λQ/m, the price impact of the constrained position divided by margin per unit, and realized volatility is fundamental volatility divided by 1 − g. For a risk model with reaction speed θ and a margin rule with elasticity γ to perceived variance, the dominant eigenvalues have modulus √(θγg*/(1 − g*)) and the market is stable if and only if θγg* < 1 − g*. The loss of stability is always a Neimark–Sacker bifurcation, so the margin cycle it creates has a period between three and four updating intervals and never a period of two... Away from that boundary the loop damps the response of margin to fundamental volatility below what the rule alone implies, and the operative risk is instead a quiet period, in which falling measured volatility lowers margin, raises leverage, and carries the gain toward the point where no price clears. A floor at √(λW) removes that possibility exactly.”

Evaluating Confidential Computing for Research Replication: A Case Study Using Intel TDX

George Mason University — Costello College of Business (Dept. of Finance) | Aarnav Bujamella, Nohith Challa, Prof. Jiasun Li
Read Publication
  • Confidential Computing Enclave Evaluation: Conducted research under Prof. Jiasun Li during the Aspiring Scientists Summer Internship Program (ASSIP, May–Aug 2025) evaluating Intel® Trust Domain Extensions (TDX) hardware enclaves on empirical replication packages from 2025 Management Science articles.
  • Multi-Language Cloud VM Benchmarking: Launched and benchmarked secure Azure and Google Cloud TDX virtual machines across hybrid analytical toolchains spanning Stata, R, SAS, MATLAB/Octave, and Python (including proprietary WRDS data workflows), tracking VM billing logs and wall-clock execution runtimes.
  • Empirical Reproducibility Findings: Achieved a ~60% exact replication rate across complete packages at a median cost of $0.29 USD (mean $0.30 USD) and a median runtime of 186 minutes (mean ~167.4 minutes), demonstrating that TDX virtual machines can securely verify multi-language financial research at negligible cost without exposing sensitive code or proprietary data.
Abstract Summary — Journal of Student-Scientists' Research (Vol. 7, 2025)
“Reproducibility in empirical social-science research is essential, yet replicating code and data from academic journals often demands complex, unsecured computing environments that editors cannot easily audit. Confidential computing technologies such as the Intel Trust Domain Extensions (TDX) hold promise to embed the entire workflow in a verifiable enclave, but their practical cost, performance, and compatibility have not been systematically evaluated. To test whether TDX can underpin scale replication, Azure and Google Cloud TDX virtual machines were launched and attempted to reproduce 2025 Management Science articles with a public or provided replication package... This yields a replication rate of ~60% at a median cost of $0.29 USD, a mean cost of $0.30 USD, a median runtime of 186 minutes, and a mean runtime of ~167.4 minutes... positioning confidential computing as a viable pathway for journals to enforce reproducibility without exposing sensitive code or data.”
